Questions parents ask

The quick answers. Anything else? Email hello@themarketjuice.com.

What is Market Juice?

Market Juice is a free daily digest that teaches kids how the stock market works. Each morning there's a market scoreboard, a few kid-friendly news stories, a word of the day, and three short games. Every game and story connects back to one of 11 core investing principles, like compound growth and diversification.

What ages is it for?

Kids ages 10 to 16. For kids 12 and under, we email the parent for consent before the account turns on.

Is it really free?

Yes. 100% free: no subscription, no upsells, no paywalls, and no ads.

How much time does it take each day?

About 3 minutes. A new digest is ready every morning at 7 AM Eastern, seven days a week. Sunday's edition wraps up the week that just ended, and Monday's previews the week ahead.

Does my kid need real money or a brokerage account?

No. Nobody buys or sells anything. Kids earn pretend Market Coins for playing, and any picks they make are practice predictions with no money involved. There's nothing to connect, link, or pay for.

What data do you collect about my kid?

Very little: the parent's email, the kid's first name and age, a username and password, game progress like Market Coins and streaks, and a few technical details such as timezone. We don't sell data, don't show ads, and don't use kids' data to train AI models. Parents can delete everything at any time. Full details are in our privacy policy.

Is this financial advice?

No. Market Juice is education, not financial advice. We explain what happened in the market and why, and teach timeless investing principles. Nothing in it is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

How is the content made and checked?

Each day's digest is drafted by AI from real market data and current news, then passes automated checks before it publishes: a kid-safe language filter, a strict format check, and guardrails on the daily puzzle. Company spotlights come from a hand-picked list of 75 well-known companies kids recognize. New glossary words only go live after a person on our team approves them. Once a day's digest is published it never changes, so every kid sees the same edition.
